# Purpose briefs for the portfolio illustrations

These briefs were written from the text next to each illustration. The visual direction favors a small explanation a reader can scan over a literal room scene: the shapes should show a relationship or process, while the surrounding prose supplies the detail.

## `img/operations-desk.svg`

### Placement and communication goal

This image sits beside “Someone has to keep track.” and the three factual work notes about managing a shift, building dashboards, and tracking keys. It should help a reader see the job as a connected operating loop: arrivals create work, work is assigned, rooms are checked, and exceptions return to a handoff or procedure. A desk, chair, and generic monitor would only repeat the setting; the useful subject is the flow of responsibility.

### Chosen art direction / generation prompt

Create a polished standalone SVG in the portfolio’s restrained isometric miniature language, on a transparent `640 × 460` canvas. Build a raised isometric routing board on the dark work-section field. Arrange four chunky stations around one continuous, easy-to-follow route: `ARRIVALS` (stacked arrival cards entering the board), `ASSIGN` (small staff tokens routed toward work), `CHECK` (a room tile with a visible check and one attention marker), and `KEY HANDOFF` (a key token moving between two handoff points). Connect the stations with broad directional rails and arrowheads so the loop reads at a glance, with the key handoff returning to the assignment/check path. Use isometric prisms, inset faces, small contact shadows, and a few human-readable words only; avoid a fake dashboard, invented counts, campus floorplan, student records, or tiny copy. Keep the visual calm and diagrammatic rather than gamified. Use `#183c48` as the dominant dark ink/structural color, `#f1f5ed` and `#c7d8d7` for surfaces and lines, and `#e6aa7e` for the exception/key accent, with sufficient contrast on the dark section. The illustration must remain transparent outside its shapes, use no external assets or scripts, and include a useful `<title>` and `<desc>`.

### Why this is the right subject

The loop makes the invisible coordination work visible. It connects the three adjacent articles without claiming a specific historical interface or outcome, and it still belongs to the established architectural model family through its plinth, solid blocks, and isometric faces.

### Recommended accessibility copy

- SVG title: `Shift handoff system`
- SVG description: `An isometric routing board connects arrivals to staff assignments, room checks, and a key handoff loop.`
- HTML `alt`: `Isometric shift routing board showing arrivals feeding assignments, room checks, and a key handoff loop.`

## `img/reading-table.svg`

### Placement and communication goal

This image sits beside “I have a theory.” and the personal essay about testing ideas, being wrong without making the mistake too costly, reconciling different interpretations, and making questions safe to ask. A stack of books and a mug would establish mood but would not explain those ideas. The illustration should show a small, repeatable feedback loop that begins in conversation and ends in a revised idea.

### Chosen art direction / generation prompt

Create a polished standalone SVG in the same restrained isometric miniature language, on a transparent `640 × 460` canvas. Build a warm clay isometric “theory test” board with three raised tiles in a circular loop: `ASK` (two speech bubbles with visibly different directions, joined by a short conversation rail), `TRY` (a small reversible test marker or bridge with a clear start/end pair), and `REVISE` (a folded idea card with an arrow returning toward `ASK`). Add one loose hypothesis card feeding into the loop, so the image reads as an idea being tested with another person and updated. Use broad arrows, clear silhouettes, and a few large short words only; do not depict a literal reading table, generic books, fake quotations, or claims about the author’s personal history. Preserve the isometric solid construction with a small plinth, raised surfaces, inset faces, and contact shadows. Use `#dea884` as the surrounding family cue, with `#302c2b` for ink, `#f7eee6` for paper/light surfaces, `#713f2d` for warm structure, and a restrained `#183c48` detail for contrast. Keep the outer canvas transparent, use no external assets or scripts, and include a useful `<title>` and `<desc>`.

### Why this is the right subject

The loop turns the essay’s point of view into a readable action: ask what someone meant, try something small, then change the explanation when reality or conversation exposes a gap. It gives the personal section a distinct purpose while retaining the portfolio’s shared architectural grammar.

### Recommended accessibility copy

- SVG title: `A theory tested in conversation`
- SVG description: `An isometric feedback loop links a question, a small test, and a revised idea through conversation.`
- HTML `alt`: `Isometric feedback loop linking a question, a small test, and a revised idea through conversation.`
